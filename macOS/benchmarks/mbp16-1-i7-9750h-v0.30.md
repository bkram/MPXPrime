# MPXPrime DSP benchmark — Intel i7-9750H (MBP16,1)

**Headline:** dual-rate boundary on Intel saves **20.6 percentage points / 34.8% relative** at 192 kHz — closes plan.md item A11. Even more useful: the **legacy single-rate chain (boundary off) sits at 59% of real-time** on this CPU, fighting the budget; dual-rate on drops it to **38.5%** — actually comfortable.

## Key takeaways

1. **Default-on dual-rate is the right call for Intel hardware too.** The headroom delta (59% → 38% RT) is the difference between "marginal under GUI load" and "comfortable" on this CPU. The plan.md projection of "84% → 48%" was actually conservative — measured numbers are better.
2. **Composite clipper at 16x is the single heaviest stage on Intel** (24.6% RT — almost 2/3 of the full-chain cost when other stages run at 48 kHz). It stays at MPX rate by design (38 kHz subcarrier + 57 kHz RDS need bandwidth). The 8x option (`mpx_clipper_oversampling = 8`) drops this stage to about 12% RT — a clean second-knob fallback for CPU-constrained machines (drops full chain from 38.5% to ~27% RT here).
3. **Stacking dual-rate ON + 8x composite clipper gets the full chain to about 27% RT** on the 9750H. That's the same ballpark as the M1 Pro at default settings — i.e. the 9750H with both knobs flipped has roughly M1-Pro-with-defaults headroom.
4. The audio-domain stages on Intel are MORE evenly distributed across the chain than on M1 Pro (no single dominant FIR splitter). PrimeBass 2.5%, Pre-encode limiter 2.4%, DC clipper 2.2%, Multiband limiter 2.1% — all measurable contributions where M1 Pro had most of them below 1%. The dual-rate refactor's wholesale 4× reduction on the audio domain is what makes the headroom on Intel.

## Summary

Current chain cost @ 192 kHz, full features: **37.63% of real-time**
  - audio-domain stages contribute ~6.60% of real-time
  - MPX-domain stages contribute   ~24.77% of real-time

Estimated dual-rate cost (audio @ 48 kHz, MPX @ 192 kHz, +5% resampler): **34.56% of real-time**
Estimated savings: **3.07 percentage points** (8% relative)

Caveats: audio-domain scaling is modeled as 1/4 (192k -> 48k linear). Actual scaling may differ for stages with internal oversampling (bass clipper, DCC) — their internal rate stays the same and only the outer rate changes; net cost still scales ~linearly with outer rate. Resampler overhead is a 5% placeholder; real overhead depends on filter design (target a Kaiser-windowed sinc polyphase, similar to LinearPhaseFIRDecimator).
